Temple Towers

Pagoda roofs in the old order, with a server farm humming under every tier. Nine levels up, twelve down, one staircase for the lot. Monks and technicians share it, and share the tea urn on the landing, and whoever empties it fills it.

The towers

The roofline is nine hundred years old. Everything holding it up went in within the last thirty.

When the valley needed cold storage for compute it needed depth, granite and water, and all three of them sat directly under the old temple. The proposal, put politely and in writing, was to move the temple. The counter-proposal came back from the abbot’s office the same week and was one sentence long: leave the temple exactly where it is and build underneath it. There is a story, probably improved with age, that it was written on the back of the original drawing.

The condition held. From Canal 7 the Towers look the way they have always looked, and old women on the bridge will tell you so with some satisfaction. From the air lanes you can see the array farms spread across the north flank, which is exactly why freight is routed east of the ridge and never over it.

Incense burns on every tier from the ninth down to the first, and the smoke finds its way out past the antenna arrays as though it were designed to. Below the first tier the air is filtered. It is the only part of the building where it is, and the technicians who work down there say they can tell the moment they come up the stair.

约定 · the agreement

Four clauses on a single page

Signed in 2058 between the abbot’s office and the compute authority. It has now outlasted both signatories and three reorganisations of the second, and it is still one page.

一

The roofline is not negotiable

Clause one

Tier spacing, order and pitch exactly as recorded in the survey of 1140. Nothing visible above the first tier except the lane beacon, which was argued into the document as a safety matter and which the abbot’s office still refers to, forty years on, as a temporary concession.

二

One stair

Clause two

No service entrance. None. Anyone going down to the halls walks in through the court, past the bell, like everybody else. The clause was the abbot’s, and it is the reason a hardware engineer at three in the morning has to cross a courtyard in the rain, and the reason the two halves of this building know each other’s names.

三

The heat comes back

Clause three

Waste heat out of the halls goes back into the hydro loop rather than into the valley air. That is why the west-cliff terraces can grow rice at this altitude, why the falls have not frozen since ’69, and why the farmers up there send tea down the hill every winter to people they have never met.

四

Same tea

Clause four

One kitchen for the entire complex, bell floor to sub −12. A clerk wrote it in as a joke, fully expecting it to be struck out before signature. Nobody struck it out. It has done more for this building than the other three clauses put together, and everyone involved now claims to have known it would.
