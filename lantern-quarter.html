Street level. The last part of the city that still measures its light in paper rather than lumens. Everything here runs off the same grid as the towers overhead, and it still burns the same red your grandmother would recognise from the far end of the street.

The quarter

The Quarter lost the argument about power in 2049, and has won every argument about shape since.

When the valley floor was rewired, the utility came down with a subsidy and a catalogue: sealed panels, uniform output, a colour temperature chosen in a room nine hundred kilometres from here by somebody who had never stood on wet flagstones in June. The Quarter took the current and sent the catalogue back. Silk over a lattice frame, filament inside — the same silhouette that hung on this street in the Ming, drinking from the same feed as the freight lanes overhead.

It took eleven years of hearings to settle. People came to those hearings straight from the workshop with glue still on their hands. What came out of it is one line long, written into the district code and nailed up at the north end of Lantern Street, where it has faded almost past reading: the grid may decide what the light costs; the Quarter decides what it looks like.

What you get is a district that is entirely modern and looks nothing of the sort. Forty-one thousand lanterns, every one of them addressable from a console upstairs, every one of them cut, stretched and glued by hand within four streets of where it hangs. Both things are true at once, and nobody who lives here finds that strange.

五条街 · five streets

What you will actually walk past

You can cross the Quarter in twenty minutes. Nobody ever has. Someone always stops you to show you something.

01

Lantern Street

Main run · 900 m

The spine of the place. Nine hundred metres of overhead line strung four deep, thick enough that the street has never had a true night, only a red one. Stand under it long enough and your hands go the colour of the light. The district code is nailed to a post at the north end: laminated, sun-bleached, never once replaced.

02

Paper Row

Workshops · 112 registered

Where the frames are cut and the silk goes on. A hundred and twelve workshops, most of them one room and one family, most of them with a radio going. Apprenticeship is still seven years, and every attempt to shorten it has quietly failed — because the last two years are not about making a good lantern. They are about what you do at eleven at night when the silk has come out wrong and the festival is on Friday.

03

The Filament Stair

Grid access · loop 7

A public stair with the district's junction boxes bolted along the wall, left in plain sight on purpose. Children learn to read the load meters here before they are trusted to hang anything. It smells of hot dust and wet stone at the same time, which is what people from the Quarter mean when they say they are homesick.

04

Wet Stone Market

Open 17:00 – tide

Named for the flagstones, which have not been properly dry since the falls were rerouted in ’68. Food, mostly, and the arguing that goes with it. The reflection off the wet stone doubles the lantern count, and the Quarter puts the reflection in the official figure. The utility contested this for years and then, one spring, simply stopped.

05

The Hanging Alley

Memorial · unlit one night a year

Sixty metres across at the top, two at the bottom, roofed over completely in lanterns. On the anniversary of the flood of ’61 it becomes the only street in 未来城 that goes dark, for one hour, on purpose. People come down and stand in it anyway. You can hear the river from there, which is the whole point.
