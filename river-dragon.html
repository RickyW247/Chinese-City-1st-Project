The River Dragon

Not advertising. Three hundred and forty metres of civic projection, coiling over the exchange district since the year after the flood — rebuilt out of fibre and fog, and still answering only to the name nobody will write down.

Altitude 340 m Signal Broadcast · permanent Uptime 26 yrs Owner of record None
无人关灯Nobody turns it off
The projection

It has never been switched off. No department will admit to owning the switch, and at this point none of them are looking very hard.

The shape up there is a scan of the stone dragon from the old river temple — the single carving that came through the flood of ’61 without a scratch, and only because it was already under three metres of water when the wall gave way. The scan was meant to be an archival record and nothing else. On the night the exchange district came back onto the grid, somebody in the reconstruction office needed a test pattern for the new emitters and reached for the nearest file. Twenty-six years later, the test has still not been concluded.

Six times a budget review has proposed taking it down. Six times the file has come back stamped owner unresolved and gone quietly into a drawer. The most recent reviewer, a careful woman who does not believe in ghosts, wrote in her own margin that she could not find a single person willing to sign the order, and that she had stopped asking after the ninth.

So it stays. Pilots read the cross-wind off the way it moves on the ridge approach. Market families steer by it without noticing they are doing it. Children are told, depending on the household, that it is a machine, or a guardian, or their great-grandmother’s opinion made visible. Nobody has yet managed to explain in writing why it is still there.

规格 · specification

What is actually up there

Filed once, in 2062, by an engineer who signed the form with a department that has not existed since 2064. It is the only technical description anyone has ever produced.

01

Source geometry

Temple carving · scanned 2061

Nine hundred years of stone, captured at sub-millimetre and never once re-modelled. Every scale in the sky is a scale a person cut by hand — including the chipped horn, which nobody has ever offered to smooth out, and which is how the old families know it is really theirs up there.

02

Emitter ring

14 towers · exchange district

Fourteen rooftop emitters, phase-locked, throwing into the valley fog that lies over the river most nights of the year. On the four or five genuinely clear nights the dragon is barely visible at all. The city files this under weather rather than fault, and the technicians take the night off.

03

Motion

Never repeats

The coil is driven by river flow — actual gauge readings from Canal 7 and the falls, arriving live. It has therefore never made the same movement twice in twenty-six years, and on the night of a release you can watch it quicken, which the market families will tell you is the river talking and the engineers will tell you is a data feed. Both descriptions fit.

04

Voice

Silent since installation

The array has an audio channel. It has never carried a sound. The technicians keep the amplifier powered anyway, pay for it out of a line item labelled ancillary, and change the subject if you ask.

05

Name

Unrecorded

The temple’s dragon had a name. It is not in the archive, it is not carved anywhere on the stone, and the families who still know it will not put it on paper for you or anyone else. Municipal documents call it Projection 7. Nobody in the valley has ever called it that out loud.

播报日志 · broadcast log

Twenty-six years, six attempts to interrupt it

Lifted out of the district maintenance record. The dragon has never gone dark on its own. These are the occasions somebody tried to help it along.

2061 · 11 November

First light

The exchange district comes back on the grid eight months after the flood, and the new emitters need something to point at. The temple scan goes up as a test pattern at 19:40. People come out onto the balconies in the rain to look at it. The test is never formally concluded.

2066 · Budget review

Decommission proposed (1 of 6)

Queried on the ledger as “unattributed lighting load”, referred to heritage, and returned marked not a structure. The clerk who wrote that phrase has since retired and says it is the finest sentence of her career.

2071 · Emitter failure

Tower 9 drops out

A phase board fails during the winter storms. The coil thins along one flank for eleven days and refuses to break. Three separate crews replace that board without a work order between them, each one certain another department must have issued it. The dragon does not flicker once.

2074 · Ownership audit

Owner unresolved

The city tries to establish title so it can sell advertising along the dragon’s length. It finds no claimant anywhere — including, after some searching, itself. The proposal lapses. The file closes without a recommendation, which in this valley counts as a verdict.

2079 · Flood anniversary

The one dimming

At the Lantern Quarter’s request, output drops by half for one hour while the Hanging Alley goes dark for the flood anniversary. It is the only deliberate change to the broadcast in its whole history, and it has happened every year since without anyone signing anything.

2087 · Tonight

Still coiling

Fourteen emitters locked, fog sitting on the river, gauge data coming in from Canal 7 every second. Status: nominal. Owner: still nobody.
