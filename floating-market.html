The Floating Market

Canal 7, under the Ming arches. Sampans stacked with printed silk and quantum tea drift under bridge-light, sold by the same families for six generations — at their own pace, indifferent to the schedule overhead.

水上买卖Trade on the water
The market

Nothing here is sold from a building. That is the whole of the rule, and the reason the market survived the rebuild.

When Canal 7 was widened for flood clearance in ’69, the plan called for a covered market hall on the east bank — climate-sealed, indexed, and thoroughly reasonable. The families who work the water declined it, on the grounds that a hull that can move is a hull that cannot be taxed as a premises. The hall was built anyway. It has been a storage depot for eighteen years.

So the trade stays where it was: two hundred-odd sampans, rafted three deep at the arches, lanterns hung bow and stern so buyers on the bridge can see what is on the deck. Prices are called up; goods go up in a basket; money comes down in the same basket. Nobody has proposed improving this.

The stock is entirely of its century. The method is not.

潮汐 · the tide

The market keeps river time

The hydro loop above the Ink Canals releases twice a night. Canal 7 rises with it, and everything on the water is arranged around that.

16:40 · Slack water

Rafting up

Hulls come down from the mooring pool and tie three deep at the second and third arches. Order is by arrival, except for the four sixth-generation families, who have the inside berths and have had them since before anyone kept a list.

17:00 · List posted

The chalk board

Tonight's goods go up at the north arch. Anything not on the board cannot be sold from the water; anything on the board must be sold at the posted price until the first release.

21:20 · First release

Prices come off the board

The canal rises about a metre and a half. Haggling opens. This is when the bridge crowds are thickest and when the baskets go up and down fastest — a good hull will clear half its deck in forty minutes.

02:45 · Second release

The tide takes the market

Water comes up far enough that the arches lose their clearance. Whatever has not sold goes back to the mooring pool. There is no closing bell; the river simply makes the point.

05:20 · First ferry

Empty water

The canal is bare from the third arch to the falls for about ninety minutes. The Lantern Quarter calls this the only quiet in the district, and comes down to it deliberately.
