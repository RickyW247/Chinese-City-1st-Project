Six quarters, and no two of them agree

Nobody here was ever made to choose between the city that was and the city that is coming. We kept both, and wired them to each other, and now they argue like family. Every quarter keeps its own records in its own hand. Push a door open and read.

Manifesto

未来城 never arrived from the future. It refused to leave the past behind, and hauled it along — glowing, complaining, thoroughly alive — into whatever comes next.

Somewhere between a temple bell and a server fan, the people here worked out a future that still knows where the good tea is. Pagoda roofs sit on server farms, and the monks let the engineers use the north stair. Sampans work the same canal they worked in the Ming, under bridges that now answer to air traffic control and, on festival nights, to nobody at all. The dragon over the skyline is a hologram. It is also, by any measure that matters to anyone living under it, the oldest thing in the valley.
