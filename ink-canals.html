水自成书The water writes itself
The canals

The engineers came for efficient channels. What they inherited were eight strokes, because that is what the old courses had always been.

The terraces were cut in the fourteenth century by a water master who was, more famously, a calligrapher. He laid his channels in the shapes of the eight strokes of 永 — eternity, the character every child in the valley still learns first — on the reasoning that water and ink turn the same corners. Everyone at the time appears to have thought this was showing off. It also worked.

When the hydro loop was engineered in ’68, the survey went up expecting to replace all of it, and came back with the awkward news that the old courses ran deeper and held better than anything the model could propose. So the loop was built along them, stroke for stroke. The reclaimed rain now falls nine hundred metres, runs the eight in order, and is caught at the weir before the river ever sees it.

At eleven the release runs full. Stand on Deck 41 with the lanterns at your back and the whole cliff face writes one character, holds it for a moment, and starts again. People who grew up here stop talking when it happens, and pretend afterwards that they did not.
