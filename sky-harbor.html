Sky Harbor

Four hundred metres up, twelve lanes wide. Freight junks folded their sails decades ago — what replaced them still bows to the wind before it leaves port, and no pilot has ever been able to say why.

The harbour

The lanes were laid out over the river because that is where the junks already went.

There was no engineering reason for it. The valley has better air two kilometres east, and the ridge approach costs every inbound hull three minutes and a fuel penalty. But the river route was the route, had been the route for six hundred years, and when the air lanes were drawn in 2044 the planners traced the old shipping charts and filed them as new.

So freight comes in over Canal 7, past the emitter ring, under the dragon's coil, and drops into the harbour arms on the north cliff. Pilots complain about it constantly and defend it furiously the moment anyone from outside the valley suggests moving it.

Twelve lanes, running to first light. Two are rerouted tonight over the falls, which is normal, and which nobody logs as a delay.

低头 · the bow

Every hull dips its nose at the ridge

It costs about four seconds and a measurable amount of fuel. It is not in any procedure, and it has never once been skipped.

01

Where it came from

River junks · pre-2044

Sail hulls dropped their bow into the wind before clearing the ridge, because the gust off the cliff face would otherwise take the rig. It was seamanship. The rigs are gone; the movement stayed.

02

What the manual says

Nothing

The lane authority has never written it down. Twice it has been proposed as a formal procedure, and twice the pilots' association has objected — on the grounds that writing it down would make it a rule, and it is not a rule.

03

What it costs

≈ 4 s · 1.2% fuel on the climb

Measured in 2081 by a consultancy from outside the valley, in a report that recommended discontinuing the practice. The report was received, filed, and thanked.

04

Who still watches for it

Deck 41 · nightly

From the balcony above the Lantern Quarter, the dip is visible against the dragon's coil. People come out for it. On a clear night, when the coil is thin, they say the ridge looks rude.
